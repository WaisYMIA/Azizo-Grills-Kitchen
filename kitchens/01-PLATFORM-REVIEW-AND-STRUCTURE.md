# Azizo Kitchens: platform review and structure (consultant memo)

Date: 22 Oct 2026. This is business and operations advice, not legal advice. Have a Kenyan advocate review sections 4 and 5 before any real cook goes live.

## 1. How the delivery platforms work in Kenya (what we copy)

| Topic | What Uber Eats, Glovo and Bolt Food do | What Azizo Kitchens does |
|---|---|---|
| Model | Marketplace: restaurants list menus, customers order in an app, a rider delivers | Same marketplace, but every kitchen sells **the same Azizo standard menu at the same price** |
| Merchant fee | Commission, typically 15% to 35% of order value (a [Kenyan restaurant guide](https://bossbot.uk/blog/whatsapp-restaurant-kenya) quotes 25% to 35%) plus optional paid promotions | **20% commission** (editable in Kitchens Admin). No joining fee in the pilot |
| Customer pays | Food price plus a delivery fee and sometimes a service fee | Food price plus a distance-banded delivery fee |
| Delivery | The platform runs the riders | Pilot: delivery is arranged by Azizo using partner riders; see section 4 |
| Merchant tools | Tablet or app to accept orders, set prep time, mark items unavailable | Kitchen Portal: accept, preparing, ready; mark dishes sold out; capacity per slot |
| Merchant checks | Business and food licences are checked at sign-up | Vetting funnel: documents, tasting, kitchen inspection, pilot period |
| Ratings | Customers rate; low-rated merchants are demoted | Customer rating after delivery feeds a kitchen scorecard; strikes lead to probation and delisting |
| Payouts | Periodic bank or mobile-money payouts | Weekly M-Pesa payout from a statement (manual in v3.0) |
| Market state | Uber has agreed to buy Delivery Hero, which owns Glovo, including its Kenyan business; the deal still needs regulator approval ([Techweez](https://techweez.com/2026/07/17/uber-buys-out-glovo-its-biggest-rival-in-kenyas-delivery-market/)). Jumia Food left Kenya in December 2023 | Expect commissions to harden if one firm dominates; owning the customer relationship matters |

Where Azizo is deliberately different:
1. **Indigenous, homemade, standard.** Platforms list whatever restaurants sell, so quality varies. We sell a small catalogue of Kenyan dishes cooked to one standard.
2. **We supply the meat.** Kitchens buy meat from Azizo butcheries. That controls quality, supports food safety traceability and earns margin before the commission.
3. **Zones.** A kitchen only serves customers inside its delivery radius (default 4 km by road estimate) and zone caps stop oversupply.
4. **Scheduled lunch and dinner slots.** Orders are batched (order by 10:30 for 12:00-14:00 lunch). Batching cuts delivery cost, which is the main reason delivery businesses lose money.

## 2. What the market tells us
Uber Eats' 2025 Kenya report names [fried chicken the top craving, then pizza](https://allafrica.com/stories/202512110269.html), which means customers already pay for convenience food. Nobody on those apps owns the "proper homemade Kenyan lunch" position. That gap is the opportunity. It is a hypothesis to prove in the pilot, not a fact.

## 3. Ray Kroc's lesson applied
The system, not the cook, produces the result. Every dish has a recipe card with weights, a packaging spec, a maximum hold time and a photo standard (see the Playbook). Kitchens may sell only dishes Azizo has **tasted and approved** for that kitchen.

## 4. Structure: how to avoid being treated as employer or franchisor
The question is how far Azizo can direct kitchens before it looks like an employer or a franchisor. Recommended structure for the pilot, for your lawyer to confirm:

**Call it the Kitchen Partner Programme, not a franchise.** A franchise has specific disclosure, fee and registration consequences. Do not use the word in contracts or marketing until a lawyer confirms the route.

**Independent business.** Each cook is an independent business that sells prepared food to customers through the Azizo platform. Azizo is the marketplace and brand. The cook is the food business owner and is responsible for their own licences, taxes, staff, premises and food.

What Azizo may require (it protects customers and the brand, and is normal for marketplaces):
- Food safety and hygiene standards, licences and medical certificates
- The standard menu, portions, packaging and labelling
- Hold times, delivery handover rules and complaint handling
- Customer data use rules and brand use rules

What Azizo should avoid (these point towards employment):
- Setting working hours or shifts, or requiring a minimum number of hours
- Controlling who the cook hires, or how they run their kitchen day to day
- Exclusivity: the cook must be free to sell elsewhere, including on Glovo or Uber Eats
- Paying a wage or guaranteed income
- Disciplining the cook as a person (instead, apply the platform rules: delist for failing quality standards)

Practical safeguards: a written Kitchen Partner Agreement (term sheet in this folder), insurance cover for product liability, an indemnity from the partner, a clear complaint and refund process, and records of every approval and inspection.

## 5. Compliance checklist for each kitchen and for Azizo
For each kitchen: business registration and KRA PIN, county single business permit, public health licence after a premises inspection, a food handler medical certificate for each person who handles food, and fire safety ([licensing summary](https://hudumaglobal.com/blog/how-to-start-catering-business-kenya-licensing-operations)). Nairobi has been [cracking down on illegal eateries](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/news/counties/nairobi-cracks-down-on-illegal-eateries-2262774), so **no kitchen goes live without these documents**, and home kitchens may need to rent a small licensed premises or get a county health ruling first. Azizo helps cooks with the paperwork; it does not skip it.

For Azizo: platforms are now regulated. A new [courier-hailing service provider licence](https://weetracker.com/2026/07/07/kenya-delivery-apps-licensing-fees-uber-bolt-glovo/) took effect on 29 July for platforms that connect customers to couriers. Ask the lawyer whether Azizo's delivery arrangement needs it, or use licensed courier partners. Customer data makes Azizo a data controller under the Data Protection Act 2019 (check ODPC registration). Issue eTIMS-compliant receipts for Azizo's commission.
